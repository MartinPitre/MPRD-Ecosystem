# SUANR_V2 Validation Test 03 — Repeated-Split Calibration Stability

## Overview

This validation test evaluates whether SUANR_V2 maintains stable and reliable split-conformal prediction-interval calibration across repeated, independently seeded train/calibration/test splits.

The test used the scikit-learn diabetes regression dataset and repeated the full modelling and calibration procedure 30 times. Each run used a different recorded seed.

**Overall result: PASS**

All 30 runs completed successfully. Mean calibrated coverage was **90.46% ± 1.27%**, closely matching the nominal 90% target, and all declared acceptance criteria were satisfied.

## Test Objective

The test asks whether SUANR_V2 can:

1. Complete at least 30 independently seeded validation runs.
2. Produce valid raw and calibrated intervals in every run.
3. Improve coverage through calibration in at least 90% of runs.
4. Maintain mean calibrated coverage between 85% and 95%.
5. Keep the mean coverage gap within 5 percentage points of the 90% target.
6. Avoid unstable or severely under-covering runs.

| Run seeds | 20260727–20260756 |

Each run first reserved 20% of the observations for testing. The remaining data were divided into training and calibration sets, producing an approximate 60%/20%/20% train/calibration/test allocation.

## Method

For each repeated split:

1. An eight-member neural-network ensemble was trained on bootstrap samples of the training data.
2. Ensemble predictions were used to construct raw 90% prediction intervals from the 5th and 95th percentiles.
3. A split-conformal correction, `q_hat`, was calculated from the separate calibration set.
4. The raw test intervals were expanded using `q_hat`.
5. Accuracy, coverage, interval-width, calibration, uncertainty, entropy, and validity metrics were recorded.

Calibration altered the prediction-interval bounds without changing the point predictions.

## Validation Decision

**PASS**

All mandatory criteria were satisfied. Across 30 independently seeded data splits, split-conformal calibration produced mean empirical coverage close to the 90% target, low coverage variability, valid intervals, and no severely under-covering run.

The result supports repeated-split calibration stability for this dataset and configuration. It does not establish cross-dataset generalization.
